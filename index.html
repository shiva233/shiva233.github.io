01 / Audio / Mixed-signal hardware

Aether

A USB-C powered audio bridge that brings Bluetooth audio from a phone or console into a PC as a USB audio input.

  • Microchip BM83 Bluetooth audio module paired with an STM32 USB interface over I2S.
  • Four-layer KiCad PCB with controlled USB 2.0 routing, ESD protection, separated power domains and an antenna keepout.
  • Custom 5 V to 3.6 V buck converter, with SWD access, configuration headers and dedicated test points for bring-up.
  • KiCad
  • STM32
  • BM83
  • I2S
  • USB 2.0

02 / Low-power / Embedded systems

Naiad

Naiad is basically a small USB-C rechargeable keychain type thing that hangs off my HUGEE water bottle and will flash to remind me to drink water when it hasn't detected a sip every X amount of minutes (in my case I chose 15 minutes). I made it because I have a really bad habit of going the entire day without drinking much water, and thought this was a fun way to solve it :)

  • Four-layer Altium PCB using an STM32U0 and BMA400 accelerometer, designed for less than 5 µA quiescent current.
  • P-FET and Schottky-diode power path for seamless switching between USB-C and an LIR2032 coin cell.
  • MCP73831 charge management and XC6135 voltage supervision for the battery's charge and discharge cycle.
  • Altium
  • STM32U0
  • BMA400
  • Battery management

03 / Product design / Embedded hardware

Project Hermes

Project Hermes is a Raspberry Pi powered game streaming handheld built for PC gamers, streaming your games from your pc via SteamLink to the console wherever you want! (provided you have an internet connection) and since its only streaming the games you can do this without rebuying games or paying console prices for the hardware!

  • Raspberry Pi 4 running SteamLink, with an RP2040-Zero USB HID controller using GP2040-CE firmware.
  • 5000 mAh UPS and USB-C passthrough charging for up to three hours of gameplay.
  • Integrated cooling and a custom enclosure, balancing power, thermals and mechanical fit within a $200 CAD budget.
  • RP2040
  • Raspberry Pi
  • USB HID
  • Fusion 360

06 / Computer vision / Mechatronics

Duck off doomscrolling

Duck off Doomscrolling (or D.O.D for short) is a fun way to quit destructive/disruptive social media! Basically it is a duck that will sit on your desk while you work, track your face using openCV and a servo, and finally.. squirt water on you if you open doom scrolling apps such as Instagram Reels! >:D (this was a hackathon project built in 4 days).

  • OpenCV face detection with sub-100 ms servo response and closed-loop targeting within one metre.
  • Two MG996R servos and a mechanically actuated water-gun trigger connect the vision system to the real world.
  • Python
  • OpenCV
  • Arduino
  • Servo control
